I built an atlas of 180 countries to settle whether India is Beveridge or Bismarck. The answer is that the question fails for most of the world: 95 of 180 countries are mixed or segmented, the three textbook models cover only 79 between them, and out-of-pocket spending is a mechanism rather than a model. India runs five pooling arrangements side by side. Segmentation is the normal case, not a transitional state, which changes what reform should be arguing about.

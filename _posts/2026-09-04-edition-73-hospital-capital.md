India's hospital groups grew revenue 21% last quarter and added 15% more working beds. Their margin did not move, staying near 23%. And ICRA expects another 14,500 beds across this year and next, at around Rs 30,000 crore. The reason margins stay flat is simple: a new bed loses money before it makes any, and it takes years to get from opening day to earning the group average. The popular fix, filling the beds, does not hold up. The link between how full a hospital is and how profitable it is points one way in FY25 and the opposite way in FY26. What does show clearly is shorter stays and higher prices per day. And KKR's $1.4bn Medicover deal is mostly a debt story, with 19 of its 25 hospitals already profitable.
